# App Store Visual Survey: what leading health apps actually look like (Sept 2026)

Method: I looked at each app's current US (QENDO: AU) App Store page in Chrome (apps.apple.com, iPhone screenshot carousel, 5–10 frames each) on 2026-09-29. Every observation below is something I saw in those images. Caveats that apply to all of it:
- These are **marketing frames**: a headline over a coloured background with a phone mock-up. The **frame** (background, headline font) and the **in-app UI** inside the phone often look different, so I describe them separately.
- **Hex values are estimates**, eyeballed from compressed JPEG screenshots with the App Store's dark page around them. Treat them as ±10% starting points, not brand specs.
- Tab bars were often cropped or too small to read. Where I couldn't read the labels I say so.
- 18 apps covered: Bearable, Visible, Guava, Flo, Clue, Stardust, Natural Cycles, Oura, How We Feel, Finch, Daylio, Headspace, Ada, Rosebud, Welltory, CareClinic, QENDO, "Endometriosis Tracker" (Digital Therapeutics) and Cysterhood. Not covered: Allara (didn't look it up), Flutter (not found in search).

## Q1. What does each app's visual design actually look like?

### Takeaway
The market splits into four visual families: (a) **warm or pastel light** (Flo lavender, Clue blush-to-red, Natural Cycles pink, Cysterhood cream, Finch sky blue, Visible's light-blue photo frames); (b) **deep dark** (Oura, How We Feel, Welltory, Stardust, Visible's in-app navy); (c) **saturated brand-colour frames** (Guava green, CareClinic royal blue, Daylio multicolour, Headspace yellow/orange); and (d) **near-raw UI screenshots** (QENDO). Serif headlines are now common among the premium-looking apps: Oura in-app, How We Feel, Ada, Cysterhood, and Clue's italic accent.

## Q2. Which visual traits separate premium-looking from cheap-looking apps?

### Takeaway
Premium in this set means restraint: one base colour plus one accent, big type in light or serif styles, generous space, a single hero object per screen, and real photography or a distinctive illustration system. Cheap means feature-dump collages, several saturated colours fighting each other, generic templates, raw screenshots and alarm colours.

### Cited Findings
- Premium examples: Oura (black frames, thin type, serif insight headlines, photo hero) — [Oura](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/oura/id1043837948); How We Feel (serif headlines, dark bg, photo journal cards, jewel-tone tiles) — [HWF](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384); Clue (blush → red gradient, cycle ring, italic serif accent, donut stats) — [Clue](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clue-period-cycle-tracker/id657189652); Visible (light sans, navy UI, mint gauge, doctor-PDF frame) — [Visible](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/visible-pacing-for-illness/id1624474919); Cysterhood (cream + green serif + coral eyebrow) — [Cysterhood](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cysterhood-pcos-weight-loss/id1638723168); Ada (pale bg, navy serif, blue keyword) — [Ada](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ada-your-health-portal/id1099986434).
- Cheap-looking examples: QENDO shows raw screenshots with no framing — [QENDO](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/qendo/id1514681675); the "Endometriosis Tracker" is a magenta reskin of the CareClinic template — [Endo Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/endometriosis-tracker/id6743563939) vs [CareClinic](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/careclinic-tracker-reminder/id1455648231); Daylio uses a different flat saturated colour on every frame — [Daylio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daylio-journal-mood-tracker/id1194023242); Guava's collages overflow with floating cards — [Guava](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/guava-health-tracker/id1622255863).

### Inferences — premium traits (most common first)
1. **A limited palette**: one dominant ground (black, cream, blush or pale blue) plus one accent (Oura teal, Clue red, Cysterhood coral, Visible mint).
2. **Serif or light-weight display type** for headlines, with a plain sans for UI (Oura, How We Feel, Ada, Cysterhood, Clue's italic).
3. **One hero object per screen**, such as a ring, gauge, big numeral or photo, rather than a grid of equal cards.
4. **Big numerals in light weight** with tiny all-caps labels (Oura "88 READINESS", Visible "5.6/14", Clue "28 days").
5. **Cards with no borders**, separated by a slight tone step or soft shadow, radius 12–16px.
6. **Real photography** (Clue, NC, Visible, Oura, HWF, Cysterhood) or **one owned illustration system** (Headspace, Finch). Stock icons don't count.
7. **Generous whitespace**: frames with only 2–4 elements.
8. **Muted, desaturated data colours** (mint, peach, lavender, teal) instead of traffic-light red/green.
9. **Consistent frame templates**: the same headline position and background treatment on every frame.
10. **Emphasis via colour or italics on a single word** (Ada blue keyword, Clue italic, Headspace orange word, Welltory bold word).

### Inferences — cheap traits
1. Raw screenshots with no framing, or visible Android chrome on an iOS listing (QENDO).
2. Heavy black serif (Playfair Black) on a saturated gradient with stacked feature lists (CareClinic, Endo Tracker). In this set, heavy serif plus loud colour reads as "template".
3. Collages of 4–6 floating cards per frame (Guava, CareClinic).
4. Rainbow category colours with no hierarchy (Daylio, Bearable's many coloured icons, CareClinic's timeline pills).
5. Alarm red/orange gauges and "CONCERNING" labels (Welltory). They're premium in craft, but anxiety-inducing for a symptom audience.
6. Dense chip walls of tiny grey text (Bearable's mood words).
7. Generic cartoon avatars (CareClinic care team).
8. Recycled UI with a condition name pasted on top (Endo Tracker).

### Gaps
- "Premium" here is my judgement from the screenshots. I found no user-perception data to back it up.

## Q3. Candidate visual directions for Atossa (INFERENCE — not observed facts)

All three directions assume Atossa's constraints: a chat/voice capture flow, a dashboard, and a "For my doctor" summary, with no diagnostic language. The last two points apply to every direction:
- Avoid Flo/Ada-style "possible causes" or condition-match patterns. Avoid Welltory-style red "CONCERNING" gauges.
- All fonts named below are on Google Fonts.

### Direction A — "Warm Editorial" (after Cysterhood, Clue and How We Feel's journal cards)
- Palette: background cream `#FBF6EF`; surface/cards `#FFFFFF`; ink `#2A2622`; secondary text `#6E655C`; primary accent terracotta/rose `#C8553D`; secondary deep sage `#2F4A3E`; soft fills blush `#F4DDD3` and sage-mist `#DDE7DF`; hairline `#EBE3D8`.
- Type: **Fraunces** or **Newsreader** (serif) for headlines and the user's own quoted words, e.g. "*It feels like a hot knife on the left side*"; **Inter** or **DM Sans** for UI and body. Headline : body ratio about 2:1 (28–32pt vs 15–16pt). Use a small letter-spaced uppercase eyebrow in terracotta (like Cysterhood).
- Cards: white, radius 16, no border, very soft shadow (0 1 2 rgba(42,38,34,.06)) or a 1px `#EBE3D8` hairline. 20–24px padding.
- Hero: a "Today's words" card, the user's most recent own-words quote in large serif italic with a small date eyebrow and one terracotta mic button. The doctor tab shows a fanned white "GP summary" document card, the same shorthand as Guava and Visible.
- Charts: a muted terracotta/sage/sand symptom-frequency strip (like Visible's PDF heat strip), not traffic-light colours.
- Risk: the old design system removed Fraunces, so this partly revisits that choice.

### Direction B — "Calm Clinical Light" (after Ada, Visible's light frames and Natural Cycles)
- Palette: background pale mist `#F3F6FA`; cards `#FFFFFF`; ink navy `#1C2740`; secondary `#5B6780`; primary accent calm blue `#3E6FD8`; secondary mint `#9FD8C0` for "logged" states; highlight peach `#F6C9A8`; hairline `#E3E8F0`.
- Type: **Source Serif 4** for headlines and Atossa's chat prompts (echoing Ada's serif questions); **Inter** for UI. The key word in each headline is in accent blue, e.g. "Tell me about your *pain*" (Ada pattern).
- Cards: white, radius 14, 1px `#E3E8F0` border and no shadow. Answer options are outlined pills (Ada) that fill blue when selected.
- Hero: a semicircular **"week at a glance" arc** (Visible-style mint gauge) showing days logged this cycle as a count, with no scores. Or a large light-weight numeral, e.g. "4 notes this week", with a tiny caps label.
- Chat: Rosebud/Ada style, with the prompt as serif text and the user's reply in sans, no heavy bubbles. Voice mode is a near-empty white sheet with a waveform in accent colour plus ✕ and ✓ round buttons (Rosebud pattern).
- Risk: it could look generic-medical. It needs one owned illustration or photo element to feel premium.

### Direction C — "Quiet Dusk" (after Oura, How We Feel and Visible's in-app navy)
- Palette: background deep plum-navy `#171A2E`; surface `#22263D`; raised `#2C3150`; text `#F2EFEA`; secondary `#A8A6B8`; accent soft rose `#E7A6A1`; secondary pale mint `#BDE5CF`; data neutral lavender `#8C8FD6`.
- Type: **Instrument Serif** or **Newsreader** for insight headlines (Oura "My Health" style); **Inter** light and regular for numerals and UI. Numerals are large at light weight (48–56pt) with 11pt letter-spaced caps labels.
- Cards: tone-step only (no borders, no shadows), radius 16–20. The tab bar is a floating rounded pill.
- Hero: a soft gradient "dusk" panel (plum → rose at the horizon, like Rosebud and Oura) with a serif line summarising the week in the user's words, plus a glowing rose mic orb as the capture button.
- The doctor summary must render as a **light** document (white PDF card) even in this dark UI, as Visible and Welltory do. That keeps it print-friendly for GPs.
- Risks: dark UIs can feel heavy for people logging pain late at night, though this may also be a plus. Dark mode was deliberately removed from Atossa on 2026-09-24, so this would reverse that decision.

### Gaps
- None of the directions has been tested with PCOS or endometriosis users.
- The hexes still need a WCAG contrast check before use. Pairs like `#A8A6B8` on `#171A2E` and white on `#9FD8C0` are likely to fail for small text.
